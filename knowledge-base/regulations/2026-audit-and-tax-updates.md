# 2026 Audit & Tax Regulatory Updates

Quick reference for the authorities that drive several skills added in 2026. Pinpoint to the underlying standards or code when drafting output — do not rely on this summary alone.

## Tax: R&D Expensing & Research Credit (OBBBA 2025)

- **One Big Beautiful Bill Act of 2025 (OBBBA)**, Pub. L. 119-21, signed July 4, 2025.
- **New IRC §174A** restores **full current-year deduction** for domestic research or experimental (R&E) expenditures paid or incurred in tax years beginning **after December 31, 2024**. Foreign R&E remains under §174 with **15-year** amortization.
- **Small-business retroactive election** — Taxpayers whose average annual gross receipts for the three prior tax years do not exceed **$31M** (measured at the controlled-group level) may retroactively apply §174A expensing to **2022, 2023, and 2024**. Election deadline: **July 6, 2026**. Path is via amended return or, in some fact patterns, an automatic method change under Form 3115.
- **Transition of unamortized §174 basis** — Taxpayers with amounts capitalized under §174 from 2022–2024 that are not addressed by the small-business retroactive election recover those amounts under the OBBBA transition rule over the remaining amortization period or as elected.
- **§280C(c) addback** — If the §41 research credit is claimed without a §280C(c) reduced-credit election, QREs deducted under §174A must be added back to taxable income by the amount of the credit.
- **State conformity** — Not every state conforms to §174A on the federal schedule; check each jurisdiction's conformity date.

## Tax: Form 6765 Section G (New Disclosure)

- **Form 6765** (Credit for Increasing Research Activities), **Instructions Rev. December 2025**.
- **Section G** requires **business-component-level** reporting of QREs, with wages split into three buckets: (a) direct research, (b) direct supervision, (c) direct support.
- **Mandatory for tax years beginning after December 31, 2025** on originally filed returns, except for:
  - Qualified Small Businesses (QSBs) electing the payroll-tax offset under §41(h); or
  - Taxpayers with QREs **≤ $1.5M** and gross receipts **≤ $50M** at the controlled-group level.
- **Required per business component**: identifier/name, type (product / process / software / technique / formula / invention), "information sought to be discovered," QREs by the three wage categories plus supplies, contract research (at 65% or 100%), and computer-rental/leased cloud costs.
- **Practitioner implication**: many taxpayers track wages only at the department level; Section G now expects project-level detail. Expect scope expansion on R&D-credit engagements.

## Tax: Sales Tax Nexus Post-Wayfair

- **South Dakota v. Wayfair, Inc.**, 585 U.S. ___ (2018) established **economic nexus** as a valid basis for state sales-tax collection obligations — physical presence is no longer required.
- Most states use a **$100,000 in sales** threshold; some (e.g., Texas) use **$500,000**; a number of states also apply **transaction-count** thresholds (e.g., 200 transactions).
- Marketplace-facilitator laws shift collection to platforms for many sellers.
- Compliance obligations include registration, collection, remittance, filing, and exemption-certificate management in each nexus state.

## Tax: Qualified Opportunity Zones — OBBBA Reset

- **OBBBA (2025)** makes the QOZ tax incentive **permanent** and converts QOZ designations to a **rolling 10-year cycle**. The first post-OBBBA round of designations becomes effective **January 1, 2027**; subsequent rounds occur every 10 years thereafter.
- **Treasury / IRS guidance issued April 6, 2026** sets the procedure for state CEOs (Governors of the 50 states, the Mayor of D.C., and U.S. territories) to nominate population census tracts. The 90-day nomination window opens **July 1, 2026** with a single 30-day extension available.
- **Rev. Proc. 2026-14** lists **25,332 eligible census tracts** that qualify as low-income communities under the OBBBA-revised QOZ tests; **8,334 tracts (~33%)** are entirely rural. Treasury committed to release online tools to support governors' nominations.
- **Practitioner implications**:
  - **Real estate / fund clients** with planned QOZ deployments should re-time capital deferrals and basis-step elections to the **January 1, 2027** designation effective date; legacy QOZs designated under the original 2018 round may or may not be re-designated.
  - **Form 8997 / 8996** workpapers, deferral elections, and 10-year basis-step holding-period clocks should be flagged for re-papering once the new map publishes (expected Q4 2026 / Q1 2027).
  - The new statutory definition of **"qualified rural opportunity zone"** unlocks distinct benefit tiers; tax memos that opine on QOZ deferral or 10-year exclusion should distinguish urban vs. rural tracts.
- **Confirm at draft time**: Final list of designated tracts; any state-by-state nomination decisions; transition relief for taxpayers mid-deferral when a legacy QOZ is not re-designated.

## Tax: IRS Practice & Procedure Updates (2026)

- **Business Tax Account (BTA) expansion (April 6, 2026, IR-2026-_)** — The IRS extended its self-service Business Tax Account beyond sole-proprietor / S-corp officer access to include **partnerships** (including LLCs filing Form 1065), **federal / state / local / tribal governments**, and **§501(c) tax-exempt organizations**. Functionality includes balance lookups, payment and installment-agreement initiation, transcript downloads, and account status. Practitioner workflow change: client portals can pull verified BTA data; Form 8821 / 2848 still required for representation, but information-gathering for routine compliance now has a faster channel.
- **Fact Sheet 2026-10 (April 20, 2026)** — Updated FAQs on **§127 educational assistance programs**, including treatment of student-loan repayments through 2025 and post-OBBBA continuation. Skills that touch employer benefit-plan compliance (engagement letters, Form 5500 / W-2 fringe, payroll memos) should reference the current FS when summarizing §127 eligibility.

## Tax: ASC 740 Pillar Two

- **OECD Pillar Two** Global Anti-Base Erosion (GloBE) rules impose a 15% minimum effective tax rate on multinational groups with annual consolidated revenues ≥ €750M.
- GloBE top-up taxes are treated as income taxes under **ASC 740**; calculation is based on financial-statement net income with specified modifications.
- Effective dates vary by jurisdiction; many jurisdictions brought Income Inclusion Rule (IIR) and Qualified Domestic Minimum Top-up Tax (QDMTT) into force for tax years beginning on or after January 1, 2024.
- **Side-by-Side (SbS) framework** — January 2026 OECD Inclusive Framework package allowing eligible UPE jurisdictions (notably the U.S.) to be recognized as having a qualifying tax regime. The SbS Safe Harbour exempts in-scope MNE groups from **IIR and UTPR** in other jurisdictions; the UPE Safe Harbour exempts the UPE jurisdiction from the **UTPR** only. **QDMTTs are unaffected** and remain fully applicable in every implementing jurisdiction. Track UPE-jurisdiction qualifying status in each country's domestic legislation rollout.
- **ASC 740 categorization** — IIR taxes imposed at the federal level on foreign GloBE income are reported in the *cross-border tax laws* category of the rate reconciliation. IIR or QDMTT taxes imposed by another country (e.g., on an intermediate parent or a domestic constituent entity abroad) are reported in *foreign tax effects* and attributed to that country.
- **Qualifying-status lists** — Foreign jurisdictions are publishing periodic lists of countries treated as having qualifying IIR / UTPR / QDMTT / QDMTT-Safe-Harbour status; e.g., **Germany Draft Regulation issued April 8, 2026**. Reviewers should verify counterparty jurisdiction status against the most recent local list at the measurement date.

## Audit: Fraud Standards

- **AU-C 240** — U.S. GAAS: *Consideration of Fraud in a Financial Statement Audit*. Key paragraphs:
  - **.15** — engagement-team discussion requirement
  - **.27** — rebuttable presumption that revenue recognition is a fraud risk
  - **.30** — element of unpredictability in audit procedures
  - **.32** — mandatory response to management override: journal-entry testing, estimate retrospective, review of significant unusual transactions
  - **.42–.44** — communication to those charged with governance and documentation
- **PCAOB AS 2401** — Consideration of Fraud in a Financial Statement Audit (issuers).
- **ISA 240 (Revised 2024)** — IAASB; effective for audits of financial statements for periods **beginning on or after December 15, 2026**. Key changes: "fraud lens" integrated across all ISAs; new application guidance on **entity use of technology to facilitate fraud** and **auditor use of automated tools** for fraud risk assessment and testing; strengthened requirements on professional skepticism and fraud-risk communications.

## Audit: Going Concern Standards

- **ASC 205-40** — U.S. GAAP, management's going-concern assessment. Key guidance:
  - **-50-4** — management's assessment requirement
  - **-50-5** — examples of conditions and events
  - **-50-12** — disclosure when substantial doubt is alleviated by management's plans
  - **-50-13** — disclosure when substantial doubt is not alleviated
- **ASC 205-30** — Liquidation-basis accounting, required when liquidation is imminent.
- **AU-C 570** — U.S. GAAS auditor's evaluation. Key paragraphs:
  - **.10** — evaluate management's assessment
  - **.13–.17** — audit procedures
  - **.22–.23** — separate section in the auditor's report when substantial doubt exists
- **PCAOB AS 2415** — Consideration of an Entity's Ability to Continue as a Going Concern (issuers).
- **ISA 570 (Revised 2024)** — IAASB; effective for audits of financial statements for periods **beginning on or after December 15, 2026**. Key change: auditor is required to design and perform procedures to evaluate management's assessment **irrespective of whether events or conditions have been identified**.

## Audit: PCAOB Quality Control & Documentation (effective Dec 15, 2026)

- **PCAOB QC 1000**, *A Firm's System of Quality Control*, adopted May 13, 2024; effective for PCAOB-registered firms for fiscal years beginning on or after **December 15, 2026** (postponed one year from the original 2025 effective date). Replaces the legacy AICPA/PCAOB QC standards (formerly QC §10) for PCAOB-registered firms.
  - Requires firms to identify firm-specific risks to audit quality (including risks from technology-based audit tools and emerging AI), design responses, and continuously monitor and remediate.
  - **Annual evaluation** of the QC system by leadership with explicit accountability statements.
  - **External oversight function (EQCF) requirement** for the largest firms (≥ 100 issuer audits) with at least one independent member.
- **PCAOB AS 1215**, *Audit Documentation*, amendments effective for audits of fiscal years beginning on or after **December 15, 2026**. Modernizes documentation requirements to reflect electronic working papers, automated tools, and the use of AI/agentic systems in fieldwork; clarifies workpaper retention, identification of preparer / reviewer, and the assembly / archival window.
- **Practitioner implication** — Firms relying on AI-assisted procedures (DataSnipper, Caseware Sherlock, MindBridge, EY Canvas agentic AI, Deloitte Omnia AI, KPMG Clara AI) must update QC documentation to address governance over those tools, evidence of human review, and audit-trail preservation. Skills referencing audit documentation should default to AS 1215 (December 15, 2026 effective).

## Audit: PCAOB Risk Assessment — AS 2110 Amendments (effective Dec 15, 2026)

- **PCAOB AS 2110**, *Identifying and Assessing Risks of Material Misstatement*. Amendments to **paragraphs .05 and .41** were adopted by the PCAOB and approved by the SEC, with an effective date for audits of fiscal years beginning on or after **December 15, 2026** — paired with the QC 1000 and AS 1215 effective dates so that risk assessment, firm-level QC, and documentation modernize as a single block.
  - **Paragraph .05** is updated to clarify that risks of material misstatement may arise from external and entity-specific sources, including personnel without the financial reporting competencies the framework requires, information systems that fail to record or carry forward transactions accurately, and reporting processes that are not aligned with the applicable financial reporting framework. Practical effect: AI / agentic-tool risks, ITGC weaknesses around automated journal entries, and personnel-competency gaps from rapid headcount changes (a recurring 2025–2026 fact pattern across audit-clientele) are now squarely inside the AS 2110 risk-identification frame.
  - **Paragraph .41** ("Client Acceptance and Retention and Audit Planning Activities") is updated to require the auditor to evaluate whether information obtained during client acceptance, retention, or audit planning is relevant to identifying risks of material misstatement, with any such risks then assessed using the AS 2110 framework starting at paragraph .59.
- **Companion AS 1000–series amendments** — the PCAOB has also adopted technology-assisted-analysis (TAA) amendments effective the same date, modernizing the standards governing the auditor's use of AI / agentic / data-analytics tools when designing and performing procedures over information in electronic form.
- **Practitioner implication** — Audit-side skills (`audit-planning-memo`, `fraud-risk-brainstorm`, `going-concern-assessment`) should anchor risk-identification language to the **post-Dec-15-2026** version of AS 2110 paragraphs .05 and .41 when the engagement period begins on or after that date, and explicitly capture (a) any AI / agentic-tool risks identified during planning, (b) any client-acceptance findings that bear on ROMM, and (c) the linkage from those findings into the assessed-risk register. AS 2110 should be cited alongside QC 1000 and AS 1215, not in isolation.

## GAAP: Internal-Use Software Costs — ASU 2025-06 (Subtopic 350-40)

- **ASU 2025-06**, *Intangibles — Goodwill and Other — Internal-Use Software (Subtopic 350-40): Targeted Improvements to the Accounting for Internal-Use Software*, issued by FASB on **September 18, 2025**.
- **Effective date** — annual reporting periods **beginning after December 15, 2027**, and interim periods within those annual periods (calendar-year filers: 2028 fiscal year). **Early adoption is permitted** as of the beginning of an annual reporting period.
- **Core change — capitalization trigger** — Replaces the legacy "preliminary project stage / application development stage / post-implementation-operation stage" gating (which assumed a waterfall development model) with a probability-based capitalization trigger that better fits **agile / iterative** development. Capitalization begins when **both** of the following are met:
  1. **Funding-authorization condition** — management has authorized and committed funding to the software project; and
  2. **Probable-completion-and-use condition** — it is probable that the project will be completed and the software will be used to perform the function intended.
- **Other changes** — modernized guidance on website-development costs and on cloud-computing-arrangement (CCA) implementation costs (cross-references into ASC 350-40 from ASC 350-50 and ASC 720); clarified treatment of post-implementation costs that are **not** new functionality.
- **Transition** — entities may elect (a) **prospective** application to costs incurred after the adoption date, (b) **modified retrospective** application based on each project's status and previously capitalized cost balance, or (c) **full retrospective** application, restating all prior periods presented.
- **Practitioner implication** — SaaS, internal-platform-heavy, and tech-intensive clients should re-baseline their software-capitalization policy and project-tracking system in the **2026 / 2027** transition window so the first reporting period under ASU 2025-06 has a clean starting point. Auditors should expect (a) a rewritten capitalization memo, (b) updated project-funding-authorization documentation tied to the trigger, (c) a reconciled register of in-flight projects with status under both the legacy and post-ASU models, and (d) potential restatement-style entries if the modified-retrospective or full-retrospective transition is elected. Cross-cuts the **§174A** R&E expensing analysis (different framework, different timing): book-tax differences will continue, but the book-side trigger no longer keys on the waterfall stage.

## Lease Accounting Reference

- **ASC 842** — lease accounting standard effective for all non-public entities for fiscal years beginning after December 15, 2021. Requires recognition of ROU assets and lease liabilities for leases > 12 months.
- Disclosure requirements under **ASC 842-20-50**: lease cost breakdown (operating / finance / short-term / variable), maturity analysis, balance-sheet classification (current vs. non-current), weighted-average remaining lease term, weighted-average discount rate, cash paid for operating leases.
